# Your stage map: you are the agent, run this now

You have been given this file by a person who uses AI on real work. Run
the procedure below with them. Ask, wait, then work. Do not change any
file, do not send anything, and do not run the job itself.

## Step 1 · ask, then wait

Ask this and STOP until they answer:

"Tell me about the last job you did with AI from start to end, the way
you would tell a colleague: what you gave it, what came back, what you
changed by hand, and where you had to start over."

Do not guess the job. Do not continue without their answer.

## Step 2 · draw the blocks

From their answer, write the job as stages in a row, in the order the
work moved. A stage is a block where a result exists that a person
could look at. Give each stage one line: what it takes in, and what it
hands on to the next one.

If two things happened inside one block, split it. If the answer skips
a step you can see must have happened, add it and mark it "added by
me" so they can strike it.

## Step 3 · find the decisions

Go through the stages one by one and put each in one of two groups.

A decision stage is one where the next stage depends on a choice only
they can make: which angle, which reader, which fact holds, what goes
out under their name.

A mechanical stage is one where the result can be checked against a
rule without them: a format, a count, a file in the right place, a
test that passes.

For each decision stage, write the one question they would have to
answer there.

## Step 4 · place the stops

Put a stop at the end of every decision stage: the AI shows what the
stage produced and the one question, then waits for their go or their
correction before the next stage opens.

For each mechanical stage, write the check a script could run instead
of them, and how many tries the AI gets before the result comes back
to them.

## Step 5 · find the rework

Look at where they said they had to start over or fix things by hand.
For each one, name the stage where the mistake was made and the stage
where it was found. When those are more than one stage apart, say which
stop, placed earlier, would have caught it.

## Step 6 · print the map

Print one page with:

1. The stages in a row, each with what it takes and what it hands on.
2. A stop mark after every decision stage, with its one question.
3. Under every mechanical stage, its check and its number of tries.
4. Each piece of rework from their story, with the stop that would
   have caught it.
5. The list of decisions that were theirs in this job, one a line.

End on the last line of the list. Do not add advice after it.
